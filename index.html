Where It Began

In 2009, Ridgeline was one tractor and no trailer, a single owner trying to make a payment. What Marcus set out to build wasn't a large fleet; it was a way to move freight that other carriers wouldn't touch, work demanding enough that doing it well was the whole point. Fifteen years later, that identity hasn't drifted, it's sharpened, even as the shape of the risk underneath it has changed.

Our Read of What You've Shared

Across your answers, a consistent shape emerges. You're pursuing an expansion into heavier, harder freight, the kind of work fifteen years of specialty experience has prepared you for. That ambition sits alongside real strain elsewhere: your own read of the numbers as "in the weeds," a team that "holds with gaps" once you step outside core dispatch, specialty knowledge you describe as living almost entirely in your own head, and systems you call fragile at any real scale. None of that is a reason not to grow, but it is the honest starting condition growth would build on. And underneath the operational picture sits a more personal one: what you've named as a concern isn't the direction itself, it's whether you can trust your own judgment on the next big call the way you did before the equipment bet came in wrong. Put together: the ambition reads as clear, the operational base it would rest on has real gaps, and the hesitation is specific, not a general loss of confidence, but a pointed question about whether to make the next bet alone.

Does that match how you'd describe it, or is there something here we've gotten wrong?
